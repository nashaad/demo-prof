# Machine Guarding and Light Curtains

Calder Ridge Manufacturing, Plant 1

## 1. Purpose and scope

This procedure defines the safeguards required on production machines and the rules for using, inspecting and reporting them. It applies to Press 3 (Line 2), Press 4 (Line 3), Conveyor C2, the CNC machines and every other machine with a moving hazard.

## 2. Fixed guards and interlocked gates

Fixed guards stay in place during all operation. Interlocked gates must stop hazardous motion when opened and must not allow restart until closed and reset. Guards may be removed only by an authorized employee under lockout/tagout (SAF-002) and must be replaced before the machine is returned to service.

## 3. Presence-sensing devices (light curtains)

Press 3 and Press 4 are fitted with Type 4 light curtains on the operator side. A presence-sensing device stops the stroke when any part of the body breaks the beam field.

- Minimum safety distance is calculated from the stop time measured at commissioning and recorded on the machine data plate.
- Press 4 stop time must not exceed 280 ms. Re-measure after any hydraulic valve or brake work.
- Operators test the curtain with the test rod at the start of every shift (see section 6).

## 4. Damaged or missing guards

A guard is damaged if it is cracked, bent, loose, missing a fastener or missing entirely. A cracked polycarbonate window on the Press 4 front guard counts as damaged.

If you find a damaged or missing guard, follow these steps:

1. Stop the machine. Use the normal stop if the machine is idle at the end of a cycle, otherwise press the E-stop.
2. Lock out and tag the machine under SAF-002.
3. Notify the shift supervisor, maintenance on call (extension 2450) and EHS (extension 2305).
4. Do not run the machine with a damaged guard. Tape, wire, temporary covers and "one more batch" are not acceptable repairs.
5. Return the machine to service only after the guard has been replaced or repaired by maintenance and inspected by EHS.

## 5. Bridging and bypassing prohibited

Bridging or bypassing a light curtain is prohibited. The same applies to interlocks, E-stops and any other safeguarding device. This includes:

- placing a jumper, bridge plug or tape across a curtain or interlock input;
- blocking or defeating a sensor with a fixture, shim or magnet;
- moving or re-aiming a curtain so that it no longer covers the hazard;
- using muting or inhibit functions outside the settings approved by EHS.

Production targets, line speed and schedule pressure are never a reason to bridge a safeguard. A curtain that trips nuisance stops is reported to maintenance for repair. Anyone who finds a bridged device stops the machine, applies lockout/tagout and notifies EHS. Bypassing a safeguard is a disciplinary matter.

## 6. Daily checks and records

At the start of every shift the operator:

1. inspects guards and gates for damage and loose fasteners;
2. tests the light curtain with the test rod at the top, middle and bottom of the beam field and confirms the stroke stops;
3. records the result on the shift check sheet.

A failed check means the machine is not used until maintenance has repaired it and the check has passed. Check sheets are kept for 2 years.
