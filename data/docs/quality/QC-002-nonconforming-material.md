# Nonconforming Material and Containment

Calder Ridge Manufacturing, Plant 1

## 1. Purpose and scope

This standard defines how nonconforming parts are identified, contained and given a disposition. It applies to raw material, work in process and finished goods on all three lines.

## 2. Identifying nonconforming parts

A part is nonconforming when it fails a dimension, visual or functional requirement in the applicable drawing or standard (see QC-001). Any employee who finds or suspects a nonconforming part tags it and informs the quality technician on shift.

## 3. Containment steps

1. Stop the process that produced the part.
2. Place the suspect parts in a red bin. Attach a red hold tag with the part number, lot, date, quantity and reason.
3. Move the red bin to the quarantine rack at the end of the line. Only quality staff may remove parts from the rack.
4. Sort back through the lot, starting from the last part that passed inspection, to find the extent of the problem.
5. Check the two most recent lots already shipped or moved to the next operation and record the result.

## 4. Disposition

The material review board meets within 24 hours of a hold. It consists of a quality engineer, a production supervisor and a maintenance representative. Each lot receives one disposition:

| Disposition | Meaning | Approval |
|---|---|---|
| Scrap | Parts are discarded and counted | Quality engineer |
| Rework | Parts are reworked and re-inspected to QC-001 | Quality engineer and supervisor |
| Use as is | Parts released with a customer-approved deviation | Quality manager |

## 5. Corrective action

A lot that is scrapped or reworked triggers a root cause review within 5 working days. The review records the cause, the corrective action and the date the action is verified. Repeat failures of the same feature within 30 days are escalated to the quality manager.

## 6. Records

Hold tags are returned to quality and filed with the lot record. Records are kept for 7 years.
