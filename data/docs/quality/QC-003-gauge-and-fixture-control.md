# Gauge and Fixture Control

Calder Ridge Manufacturing, Plant 1

## 1. Purpose and scope

This standard controls the calibration of measuring equipment and the use of inspection fixtures so that measurements are repeatable. It applies to equipment used by Quality and to torque wrenches used for fixture assembly.

## 2. Calibration intervals

| Equipment | Interval | Method |
|---|---|---|
| Digital calipers | 6 months | Gauge blocks |
| Three-point bore gauges | 3 months | Setting ring |
| Torque wrenches | 6 months | Torque analyzer |
| CMM | 12 months | Certified artifact |

Each item has a unique gauge ID and a label showing the calibration date and the next due date. Equipment past its due date is removed from use and tagged red.

## 3. Handling and storage

Gauges are stored in their cases at the quality lab or the line gauge crib. Do not drop, force or measure hot parts. Report any gauge that is dropped, damaged or reads differently to the quality technician for a calibration check.

## 4. Inspection fixtures

Inspection fixtures hold parts for CMM and bench measurement. Each fixture has a fixture number, a drawing and a datum certification valid for 12 months. A fixture with damaged locating pins or a bent base plate is red-tagged.

## 5. Fixture clamp torque

Fixture clamp FC-210 is used on inspection fixtures and on production pallet fixtures. Torque the M10 clamp bolts on inspection fixtures to 50 Nm with a calibrated torque wrench so that the datum position stays repeatable between measurements. Tighten in a star pattern and mark each bolt with a torque stripe.

Check the stripe before every measurement session. A broken stripe means the fixture must be retorqued and the first part re-measured.

## 6. Records

Calibration certificates and fixture datum certificates are kept for the life of the equipment plus 3 years.
